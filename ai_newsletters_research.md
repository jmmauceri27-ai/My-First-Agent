## CATEGORY 1: HIGH-VOLUME DAILY BRIEFINGS (General AI News)

### 1. The Rundown AI
- **Publisher:** Rowan Cheung (bootstrapped)
- **Website:** therundown.ai
- **Frequency:** Daily
- **Audience:** General tech professionals, AI enthusiasts, business users
- **Content:** Daily AI news, tool launches, product releases, embedded "how to use it" tutorials
- **Subscribers:** 2,000,000+ (~50% open rate — nearly double the 31% industry average)
- **Cost:** Free
- **Notable:** Rowan Cheung has interviewed Sam Altman, Mark Zuckerberg, and Satya Nadella; launched a companion EdTech platform

---

### 2. TLDR AI
- **Publisher:** Dan Ni (TLDR Network)
- **Website:** tldr.tech/ai
- **Frequency:** Daily (Mon–Fri), ~5-minute read
- **Audience:** ML engineers, AI infrastructure teams, technical PMs, data scientists
- **Content:** AI/ML research papers, product launches, industry news — dense bullet-point summaries
- **Subscribers:** 1,250,000+ (TLDR Network as a whole: 7M+ across 9 editions)
- **Cost:** Free (ad-supported)
- **Notable:** Dan Ni grew this from a side project in 2018 into a profitable 8-figure media company with 22 remote employees

### 3. Superhuman AI

- **Subscribers:** 1,000,000+

### 4. The Neuron

- **Notable:** Grew from zero to 500K+ in under two years; expanded into a podcast and educational platform serving 20,000+ students

## CATEGORY 3: TECHNICAL / AI RESEARCH (For ML Engineers & Researchers)

### 8. Ahead of AI
- **Publisher:** Sebastian Raschka, PhD (Senior LLM Research Engineer, Lightning AI; former UW-Madison professor)
- **Website:** magazine.sebastianraschka.com
- **Frequency:** ~2x/month; each issue is a 30–90 minute deep read
- **Audience:** ML researchers, LLM practitioners, AI engineers wanting architecture-level understanding
- **Content:** LLM architectures, post-training methods, curated paper roundups with diagrams, code references, citations
- **Subscribers:** 190,000+
- **Cost:** Freemium (free + paid Substack)
- **Notable:** The largest deep-technical AI newsletter; explains the *how* behind model releases, not just the *what*

## KEY OBSERVATIONS

1. **Scale polarization:** The market has bifurcated between mega-newsletters (The Atlas at 3M+, The Rundown at 2M+) and high-quality niche publications (Interconnects at 60K, Gradient at tens of thousands). Niche newsletters often have higher engagement and more influential readership.

2. **LinkedIn as a major channel:** Three newsletters in the top 10 by subscriber count (The Atlas, AI Frontier, Learn AI Together) exist primarily on LinkedIn, reflecting the platform's growing role as a B2B content distribution channel.

3. **Freemium is the dominant model:** Nearly every newsletter uses a free-core / paid-extras model. Main exceptions are Stratechery (primarily paid) and AI Supremacy (primarily paid), which use paywalls as quality signals.

4. **Convergence with podcasts:** Latent Space, The Neuron, Eye on AI, and the CAIS Newsletter have all launched companion podcasts — newsletters are becoming multi-format media brands.

5. **Policy and safety are growing:** Several newsletters gained major milestones in 2025–2026 specifically covering AI governance, safety, and regulation, reflecting maturation of the policy debate post-EU AI Act.

6. **Institutional entrants:** MIT Technology Review launched a new AI-specific newsletter ("Making AI Work") in February 2026, signaling major legacy media institutions competing in the AI newsletter space.
